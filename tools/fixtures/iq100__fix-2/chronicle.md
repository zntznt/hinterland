# A Chronicle of the Hinterland

*Kemerby keeps this record of the world called "fix-2", closed in 1250 while Brack reigned.*

## The Founding, Year 1000

The survey that opens this record counts 12 regions and one capital at Kemerby; the south wind and the shape of the rock between them account for most of this record before anyone in it made a choice.

Nobody sized the towns: the largest closed the founding at 11508 and the middling one at 2478, and what a town already had is what it got more of, which is how 11508 pulled that far clear.

The refining is done at Kemerby and consumed where it is made, and the founding left 5 settlements dark, on an arithmetic nobody in them was shown.

Kemerby has no lane and no aerie to reach: nowhere in the realm was the cargo valuable enough or the ground hard enough to justify the stones, though so every crossing between these 12 regions is made on the ground, at the ground's price.

Volkruk Teeth and Molgrek Teeth run across the country and Skal Pass and Bellok Steps are the only way over, though 3 regions sit in the mountains' shadow, cut off from Kemerby by the wall, and it costs them.

The charts put Denmere Expanse off the north coast. And Pellness Harbor won the founding lottery, and the winning ticket was a shoreline.

The Merby Rill runs down from the high ground by Varrgak through 2 regions to the border: no charter allocated the Merby Rill, and the allocation is the steepest thing in this record.

Skavdurk, a delve past Skulgruk, has never been surveyed to the bottom, Lyanth was buried by Varrgak before the founding and has not been disturbed and Korvantgrek, a delve past Vurzekmouth, has never been surveyed to the bottom, and none of it is on any charter, and delving pays at Varrgak when nothing else does, and it is priced accordingly.

The realm's edge at Raven is where Ulvere Quay was allowed to happen: Ulvere Quay is not a failure of the realm's rules, it is what the rules pay for.

There are towers at Raven Tower that no charter permits and no writ reaches; one tower stands against the whole of Kemerby's writ, and the writ has not reached it.

Varrgak Bridge is the only bridge in 12 regions. Whoever came to hold the only span in 12 regions did not have to build it.

## The Years

Cut where the record turns, the years give **the Age of the Gates** (1000–1100), in which the gap did not move, **the Still Years** (1125–1200), when nothing in the spread moved and **the Emptying** (1225–1250), in which the gap did not move. And they are cut where the wealth turned, which is the only boundary the 3 ages actually have.

**Year 1025.** The water closed on Pellness for reasons made elsewhere. Pellness had no say in the quarrel and paid the whole of the settlement. Pellness discovered what a fortune built on somebody else's politics is worth.

**Year 1025.** Whatever the old world left under Skulgruk broke loose; the Temple sealed the site at Skulgruk and wrote the account. The Temple's account of Skulgruk was written by the Temple.

**Year 1050.** The ground at Hamwiceweld was suddenly worth the trouble, and the road to Hamwiceweld was repaired within the year, which no petition had managed in fifty. Nothing about Hamwiceweld had changed except what was known about it; the clerks called it the Hamwiceweld Find without saying what it was.

**Year 1075.** Diggers hit rich ore under Hamwiceweld where nobody had thought to look: the rush was immediate: retainers, chancers, assayers, and everyone who trails them into Hamwiceweld.

**Year 1075.** Kemerby was where two claims came to blows. The Crown and the magnates did the fighting, and Kemerby did the dying. What Kemerby lost was a third of its people and a quarter of its coin, so it is filed at Kemerby under the War of 1075.

**Year 1075.** There was a funeral, and then Ulverfolt held the throne: the entry at Kemerby runs to one line, and that is generous. The 12 regions were informed by notice that Ulverfolt had the title.

**Year 1075.** After years of refusal, a Tariff Amnesty came out of Kemerby. The fees at every gate were capped by decree out of Kemerby, and the gates were left standing. The 12 regions had asked for it before it was cheap to grant.

**Year 1100.** The Hierarch died and the charter answered the question; Sarelle took the arrangement as found and left it as found. Across 12 regions the only thing that changed was a name, and the name is Sarelle.

**Year 1100.** The paper that ended it was signed at Kemerby: on paper and in the Crown's hand, the magnates ceded nothing but their claim and kept their ledger, which held little. The paper at Kemerby records a balance of ledgers and calls it a peace: the copy at Kemerby is titled the Peace of Kemerby.

**Year 1100.** Hamwiceweld was ill before it was infected. And those who could walk left Hamwiceweld, and those who could not were counted after. Nothing came to Hamwiceweld from Kemerby except the assessment, on time: the clerks called it the Dust Fever of 1100 because the year needed a name.

**Year 1100.** Skulgruk broke the constabulary line and kept the town. And what Skulgruk makes stays at Skulgruk. What the charter had held back at Skulgruk ran at full tilt the moment the charter went. The people keep the date as the Skulgruk Rising.

**Year 1125.** The Dominion took Pellness Harbor and did not have to fight for it. And nothing at Pellness Harbor was destroyed, and everything at Pellness Harbor changed owner. Those 7 regions are the best-connected and worst-paid ground in the realm; it is entered as the Landing at Pellness in a language Pellness does not use.

**Year 1125.** Nothing fell on Sellowford for two summers together; grazing around Sellowford failed a season before the grain did. Nothing at Sellowford was rationed, which is a decision even when nobody makes it. The dry page is headed the Drought of 1125.

**Year 1175.** Kemerby lost its aetherworks in 1175. What Kemerby had instead of a future was a seam. The charter for Kemerby is still on file and refers to nothing standing.

**Year 1175.** No claim was pressed against Santhe, so Santhe took the censer. And nobody in the 12 regions was consulted, and nobody expected to be. The 12 regions kept paying through it, to Santhe instead.

**Year 1175.** When the First Magnate died the question of the chair was open, and it stayed open; Krumth held the chair at the end of it and holds it still. The title at Kemerby changed to Krumth and the arrangement did not.

**Year 1175.** The river went over its banks at Vurzekmouth: the ground that floods at Vurzekmouth is the only ground the poor could afford. The survey had the Vurzekmouth floodline on it and the leases ignored it. The accounts call it the Vurzekmouth Flood, and they do not agree on how many it took.

**Year 1200.** The rains did not come to Sellowford in 1200, nor the year after. And what water there was went where it had always gone, which at Sellowford meant uphill. Nothing at Sellowford was rationed, which is a decision even unmade: the Drought of 1200 is what the dry page is headed.

**Year 1200.** Vurzekmouth was under water from the wharves to the second street; the survey had marked it and the leases at Vurzekmouth were signed anyway. The count of the dead at Vurzekmouth is an estimate and always was, and it is the Vurzekmouth Flood in the register and something shorter in Vurzekmouth.

**Year 1200.** Something let go beneath Markholt in 1200, and the pass above Markholt was days clearing, and the tariff on it was collected throughout. What the ground did at Markholt it had done before the realm was surveyed. And the register carries it as the Markholt Quake.

**Year 1225.** Kemerby chartered aetherworks at Pellness. And Pellness was wired before it was paved. The copper to Pellness was laid faster than any road to Pellness ever was.

**Year 1225.** The throne passed quietly to Brack. And what Brack inherited was the arrangement and not the choosing of it. Neither the ledger nor the map at Kemerby records that Brack arrived.

**Year 1225.** The rains failed over Sellowford, and failed again, so the country the water had barely reached went to dust first, as it always does around Sellowford. The water that was left at Sellowford went where water always goes, which is to whoever owns the channel, and the year went into the register as the Drought of 1225.

**Year 1225.** The river went through Vurzekmouth rather than past it; the Vurzekmouth wharves were rebuilt at public cost and the houses were not. Nobody at Vurzekmouth was compensated, because nobody at Vurzekmouth had insured anything; the clerks filed it under the Vurzekmouth Flood and moved on.

**Year 1225.** The ground moved under Markholt, where the wall's own folding runs closest to the surface, so the pass above Markholt closed, and with it the only route that did not pay a tariff. The masons at Markholt had said which streets would go and had not been asked twice, so the year is filed as the Markholt Quake.

**Year 1225.** What the surveys had walked past at Hamwiceweld was found by accident: for one generation Hamwiceweld was worth going to. The road to Hamwiceweld was repaired inside a year, which fifty years of petitions had not managed: the clerks file it as the Hamwiceweld Find.

**Year 1250.** The Hierarch left an heir and a rival, which is to say no heir at all. And what settled it was not law, and Novare is who it settled on. Novare inherits a Hierarch's ledger and none of a Hierarch's choices.

**Year 1250.** The fold under Markholt moved for the first time in living memory. The count at Markholt was taken by the people who survived to take it. The pass above Markholt was the cheap route, and it was days before it was a route again, and the Markholt Quake is the entry, and it does not mention the ground.

Bynames settled on the places the years used hardest: Kemerby the Yoked, Skulgruk the Free, Sellowford the Yoked, Merholt the Shadowed, Vurzekmouth the Hollow, Varrgak the Yoked, Markholt the Hollow, Raven the Lofted, Asdenby the Yoked, Hamwiceweld the Yoked, Thereby the Yoked and Pellness the Yoked, so nothing in Kemerby authorised those 12 names and nothing in Kemerby can withdraw them.

## The State of the Realm, Year 1250

Counted at the close, the 9 settled regions divide: 1 ended richer than at the founding, 7 ended where the founding left them and 1 lost ground, and those 3 deadholds were not closed, they were left.

Skulgruk, Vurzekmouth and Markholt are deadholds now: 3 holdings stand where towns used to.

9 regions to the Crown against 1 to the Temple and 0 to the magnates. 1 is contested between them, and 1 answers to no one at all.

There are 5 gates in the realm and they are held 3 by the Crown, 0 by the Temple and 0 by the magnates, though not one of the 5 gates was chosen by the people who pay at it.

What the gates collected went mostly to the Crown, so the loop closes on itself: the Crown and the Temple are simply the two nearest to acting on it.

The sharper division is not between towns but inside them, where 3.7 of the people hold 35.9 of the coin. And they live 14.7 times better than the people who work for them, and the gap is sharpest at Kemerby, where 64 coins in every hundred belong to the few.

7 of the realm's regions answer across the water now, though the Crown still reigns, and it no longer rules the 7 regions the Dominion holds, and the two are not the same thing.

1 settlements are dark at the close and were dark at the founding. And the wire goes where a ledger says it repays, and 1 settlements do not.

## What the Record Shows

What the survey of 12 regions supports, and nothing beyond it. This world got more unequal, from 0.27 at the founding up to 0.36: 0.36 is an ordinary reading for this ruleset. A Tariff Amnesty in 1075 is where the curve bends. The run does not read as one period but as 3: the Age of the Gates (1000–1100, gini 0.29 → 0.31), the Still Years (1125–1200, gini 0.30 → 0.31), the Emptying (1225–1250, gini 0.35 → 0.36), and so the 3 ages are cut from the same epoch series the timeline draws, so the two surfaces cannot disagree. The poorest fifth of this realm's towns carries 1.0× the blight of its richest fifth, and so at 1.0× the burden is not sorted by wealth at all. Spatial clustering returns 0.400 for wealth and -0.254 for blight over the region adjacency; the smoothing kernels that lay down the terrain guarantee some of this before any economy runs, so the number describes the map's construction as much as its history (p 0.020 and 0.135, 199 permutations). A median settlement behind the Volkruk is 95% poorer than one the same distance out in the open, though the Volkruk is the only term left once distance is controlled for. Vurzekmouth stands at the river mouth and takes 5 points of other towns' poison with the water, and so being last on 12 regions' river is not a policy anyone adopted. 8 regions are charged at crossings they do not own; there is no route around the 4 of them; that is what makes them worth holding. One side of the Volkruk gets 38 and the other 2. The farms went where the water was, 38 against 2, and the wind was not consulted. The size distribution came out rank-size, tail slope α 1.02, fit 0.86. Proportional growth guarantees a size law before any economy runs, so read the slope and not the line: fit 0.86, primacy 1.2×. Measured in distance saved, the skyway gives the shadow 57% and the open country 0%, and a lane that saves 57% is a lane priced beyond the people it would save it for.

Nothing above was authored. It fell out of the ore, the wall, the water and what the ledgers said would pay, across 10 epochs.
