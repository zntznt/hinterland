# A Chronicle of the Hinterland

*The record of the world called "fix-3", kept at Comfre and closed in 1250 under Larkholl.*

## The Founding, Year 1000

12 regions, one capital at Comfre, and this account of both, so where the ore sits and where a wagon can climb explain more of the next 12 pages than any decree does.

At the founding the biggest town held 12856 and the middling one 3050. Centuries of it across 12 regions: good land paid, trade drew, and growth went to whatever was already growing.

The refining is done at Comfre and consumed where it is made. The founding left 6 settlements dark, on an arithmetic nobody in them was shown.

Above all of it runs the Tanneyholt Lane, lift-barges between Comfre and Mariellenne Delf that cross what the roads must go around; a fare at the aerie buys what the Tanneyholt saves, and the saving was never going to be shared.

The Vrathul Teeth, Zolgrak Teeth, Skornathul Teeth and Rumkelth Teeth wall off the country, and the roads across go through Tral Stair, Krak Stair and Drak Pass, and the wall puts 6 regions behind it, further from Comfre than the map alone would say.

Open water lies to the south, and it is drawn as Brook Waters, though the outward trade of the realm goes through Comfre Harbor and pays there.

3 regions take their water from the Lindenholt Beck, which starts above Osterewick. No charter allocated the Lindenholt Beck, and the allocation is the steepest thing in this record.

What came before has not gone: the delve called Zarrek gapes in the old workings by Gruggath, Kyrisiel was buried by Drennath before the founding and has not been disturbed and the deadhold of Santhe stands empty by Mariellenne Delf, and its ground is poisoned yet. Delvers work the Mariellenne Delf road every season because it pays when nothing else does, not all of them come back, and what they carry out is sold off the books.

Wolt Quay keeps the water past the last stone by Mariellenne Delf, on nobody's charter, and Wolt Quay exists because the realm's own rules made it profitable, which is the only reason anything exists here.

Lorne Refuge stands above Surzethrak on ground no authority granted. Pilgrims walk to Lorne Refuge alongside the official roads, which the Temple is reminded of every festival.

The realm's crossings are Quillton Bridge. The alternative to the single span is a ford, and across 12 regions the fords are counted in wagons rather than in tariffs.

## The Years

Read at a distance the years are **the Quiet Years** (1000–1025), when the distance shrank, **the Gated Age** (1050–1175), when what separated the top from the middle grew and **the Quiet Years** (1200–1250), in which the gap did not move. And nothing named those 3 periods but the shape of the record: the pile-ups, the levies, and the towns that went dark.

**Year 1025.** A quarrel between the Vantreth Imperium and the Metropole became the ruin of Comfre, so Comfre had built on foreign trade, and foreign trade was withdrawn. The capital that closed the lanes has never heard of Comfre.

**Year 1025.** The stones set at Mariellenne Delf were the only thing that stayed, and the survey that placed Mariellenne Delf read the water and the road and did not read the ground.

**Year 1025.** The hamlet marked at Drennath was gone before the first count, and the ground is the reason, so an entry in the survey of Comfre is not the same thing as a town.

**Year 1025.** Nobody wintered at Surzethrak: the site was never one people could hold. The rolls of Comfre carried it for a season and then stopped.

**Year 1025.** The magnates seized the crossing at Comfre in 1025, so the magnates collects at Comfre and maintains nothing. Nobody who crosses at Comfre was asked, and nobody who crosses there has another way round.

**Year 1025.** The rains failed over Comfre, and failed again; the country the water had barely reached went to dust first, as it always does around Comfre. The grain price at Comfre doubled and the wage did not, so the Drought of 1025 is the entry, and it is a short one.

**Year 1025.** In 1025 the fever reached Comfre and did not leave, and the dead at Comfre were counted where they could be counted. The blight at Comfre was decades old and nobody had been charged for it; the Dust Fever of 1025 is what the roads out of Comfre still call that year.

**Year 1025.** The fever found Quillton on ground already poisoned, and the blight had sat heavy on Quillton for years, so the fever arrived to a town already half-poisoned. Those who could leave Quillton left, in the order their savings allowed. And the clerks called it the Bog-Rot of 1025 because the year needed a name.

**Year 1050.** Comfre watched its ground go pale and then go hard: the wells that had been common at Comfre stopped being common. Nothing at Comfre was rationed, which is a decision even when nobody makes it, and the clerks headed the year the Drought of 1050.

**Year 1075.** The aetherworks at Comfre failed in 1075; what the ledgers built at Comfre in a generation they unbuilt in a year, and nobody took the copper up. What Comfre was built for lasted one lode.

**Year 1075.** The Crown arrived at Comfre with a document and left with a gate; the tariff post at Comfre went up before the claim was answered. The 12 regions gained no road that year and lost a free one.

**Year 1075.** Comfre hardened rather than yielded; dumping stopped being a practice at Comfre and started being a policy. The 12 regions were informed rather than consulted.

**Year 1075.** The Temple consecrated the ground at Comfre as Jesseriel in 1075: the road to Comfre carries pilgrims where it used to carry the people leaving. What happened at Comfre is now a pilgrimage, which is one way of not answering for it.

**Year 1100.** Prospectors struck a hidden lode under Surzethrak; assayers, chancers and retainers were at Surzethrak inside a season.

**Year 1100.** When the First Magnate died the question of the chair was open, and it stayed open, and the rivals to Dhordruk are not named here, which is itself a kind of record. The 12 regions were informed by notice that Dhordruk had the title.

**Year 1100.** The country around Comfre dried from the edges inward. The price of water at Comfre was set by the people who had some. The drought at Comfre was a shortage for some and a market for others, so the Drought of 1100 is the name and the whole of the explanation.

**Year 1125.** Aetherworks were put up at Comfre because the assay said so, so everything Comfre got, it got because the ore was worth it. The towns the trunk line passed on its way to Comfre are not in the charter.

**Year 1125.** The Sovereign died in 1125 and the succession went as written, to Evere. The 12 regions carried on as though nothing had happened, because nothing had. The ledger at Comfre did not notice that a Sovereign had become Evere.

**Year 1125.** A claim was filed on the Comfre crossing and enforced the same season, so the notice went up at Comfre and the queue formed under it the same week. Gates are the cheapest thing in the realm to take and the dearest to be under, at Comfre as anywhere.

**Year 1125.** The fold under Drennath moved for the first time in living memory: roads cracked, the pass shifted, and what stood on soft ground at Drennath did not stand after. What the ground did at Drennath it had done before the realm was surveyed, and the record keeps it as the Drennath Quake.

**Year 1125.** The pilgrim traffic found Comfre again: nothing was built at Comfre and everything was worth more. The Temple's share of what came to Comfre was taken first. And the Temple keeps the year as the Rise of Comfre.

**Year 1150.** The Vantreth Imperium took an interest in Comfre Harbor and said so politely: the capital watched and recorded nothing about Comfre Harbor. An invitation costs less than a fleet, and Comfre Harbor is being invited.

**Year 1150.** The old Hierarch died, and Rosane took the censer without incident, and the treasury at Comfre paid for a funeral and for nothing else. Comfre kept the date and the name Rosane and dropped everything else.

**Year 1150.** The wells at Mariellenne Delf were reading low by midsummer and dry by the next. And what Mariellenne Delf had stored was gone by the second summer. The herds around Mariellenne Delf were sold at whatever was offered, so it survives as the Drought of 1150, three lines long.

**Year 1150.** The rock under Drennath shifted, as rock under a fold does; the survey had said what the ground under Drennath would do. The fold runs the length of these 12 regions and Drennath sits on it. And the Drennath Quake is the entry, and the entry is short.

**Year 1150.** The weather turned on Comfre in 1150 and stayed turned, so what Comfre lost was a season, and a season is what most of it lives on. What Comfre lost was a season, and a season is what it lives on. And the Great Storm of 1150 is the name the survivors gave the year.

**Year 1150.** Surzethrak broke the constabulary line and kept the town. What Surzethrak makes stays at Surzethrak. People came to Surzethrak from the tariffed country around it, which is the plainest verdict available; the Surzethrak Rising is the date they keep, and they keep it without permission.

**Year 1175.** The country under Drennath moved without warning, so the masons at Drennath had known which streets would go, and had not been paid to say so. The masons at Drennath had said which streets would go and had not been asked twice. And the Drennath Quake is the entry, and it does not mention the ground.

**Year 1200.** Larkholl succeeded the old Sovereign and nobody contested it, so no gate changed hands over it, which the clerks at Comfre noted as unusual. What Larkholl inherits across 12 regions is a ledger, and it did not move.

**Year 1225.** Vralgrok came to the chair by the ordinary route: nobody in the 12 regions was consulted, and nobody expected to be. Vralgrok inherits a First Magnate's ledger and none of a First Magnate's choices.

**Year 1225.** Quillton was under water to the second street, and everything stored at the Quillton wharves was a loss, and the stores were the year. The survey had the Quillton floodline on it and the leases ignored it: it is the Quillton Flood in the register and something shorter in Quillton.

**Year 1225.** The sea came at Comfre for three days without stopping: the fleet at Comfre was insured and the crews were not. The season is what Comfre lives on, and the season was the loss. And the year is kept as the Great Storm of 1225.

11 places now answer to what happened to them: Comfre the Mourning, Gruggath the Open, Tarkfiere the Shadowed, Quillton the Mourning, Mariellenne Delf the Hollow, Drennath the Hollow, Osterewick the Shadowed, Wordmere the Open, Surzethrak the Free, Coltby the Shadowed and Tarrewood the Shadowed. And those 11 bynames were granted by no charter and can be lifted by no decree, which makes them the plainest record in this document.

## The State of the Realm, Year 1250

The 10 settled regions came out of the years as follows: 1 ended richer than at the founding, 6 stayed put, 2 slid and 1 failed, though the roads to those 2 deadholds were never closed, they were simply stopped being taken.

What the years emptied comes to 2 holdings.

Mariellenne Delf stands on ground the record had already closed. And it stands under a name in a different tongue than the one the Comfre charts carried before.

The emptiest place in the record is Mariellenne Delf, which the roads have given up on.

The realm divides 9 to the Crown, 1 to the Temple and 0 to the magnates. A further 1 of the 12 regions acknowledge no authority at all.

The bridges, passes and quays number 5: Crown 3, Temple 0, magnates 1. The one that charges nothing charges nothing because nobody found it worth holding, and the other 4 do.

The Crown came out of the years with the deepest ledger: what the gates earn buys more gates, and of the powers the Crown and the magnates are the pair worth watching.

The division that decides most lives here is 3.6 against 31.4, and it is inside the towns. And the ratio is 12.4 to one, owner to labour, and at Comfre it reaches 56 coins in the hundred.
