# A Chronicle of the Hinterland

*Asellby keeps this record of the world called "fix-6", closed in 1250 while Lasellmoor reigned.*

## The Founding, Year 1000

12 regions, one capital at Asellby, and this account of both, and the weather comes west and the rest came from underneath: the ore, the gradient, and what a wagon can climb.

At the founding the biggest town held 14917 and the middling one 2395: what a town already had is what it got more of, which is how 14917 pulled that far clear.

The refining is done at Asellby and consumed where it is made; 4 settlements started off the grid: reachable by road, but with no power line, because the ledgers said wiring them would not pay.

Between Dravzek Crag and Asellby the Merell Lane carries what will pay the lift. You pay at the aerie to board the Merell, so the sky belongs to whoever can afford the fare while the road belongs to everyone.

Dhulzar Teeth and Drennak Teeth stand across the realm and the roads must find Vrak Stair to get over; 5 regions are on the far side of it, further from Asellby in cost than in distance.

Open water lies to the east, and it is drawn as the Mermook Main. And the realm's gate is Hamwick Harbor: everything the mines raise and the aetherworks refine leaves through them, and whoever holds the quay collects the tariff.

2 regions take their water from the Briar Rill, which starts above Dravzek Crag, though the towns drink from it in order, so Dravzek Crag gets it clean and Ramwill Reach at the mouth gets whatever every town and aetherworks above has put in it.

The realm is built over an older one, and Kalgrek, a delve past Dravzek Crag, has never been surveyed to the bottom, the tomb of Nivelianthe keeps its silence in the barrens by Serrvarnuk Bastion and the old workings by Ramwill Reach are open at Gnakkelkrak and nobody has closed them: delvers work the Ramwill Reach road every season because it pays when nothing else does, not all of them come back, and what they carry out is sold off the books.

Asten Quay sits past the last stone by Brackby, on no charter and no map the capital keeps. And Asten Quay exists because the realm's own rules made it profitable, which is the only reason anything exists here.

The climb above Serrvarnuk Bastion ends at Ilyse Refuge, which was sanctioned by nobody, so nobody healed at Ilyse Refuge appears in any count the realm keeps.

The bounty country keeps Skaldek Camp by Dravzek Crag and keeps no records. And the stands thin the predation on that ground, the trophies are fenced where nothing is taxed, and for the poorest of the realm's 12 regions the bounty is the one rung of a ladder nobody built.

Ramwill Reach Bridge and Dravzek Crag Bridge carry every crossing in the realm, though the 2 spans were built by the realm and are held by whoever got there first.

## The Years

2 ages carry the years between them: **the Age of the Gates** (1000–1050), when the gap narrowed and **the Age of Tariffs** (1075–1250), when what separated the top from the middle grew. The 2 boundaries are where the wealth turned, and nothing else marks them.

**Year 1025.** The sea closed on Hamwick in 1025, and the second fortune at Hamwick was made on trade and unmade the same way. Nothing at Hamwick was destroyed and everything at Hamwick stopped.

**Year 1025.** Nobody wintered at Khekton: the site was never one people could hold, so the rolls of Asellby carried it for a season and then stopped.

**Year 1025.** Serrvarnuk Bastion appears in the founding survey and in no census after it. And no one left Serrvarnuk Bastion, because no one was ever there to leave.

**Year 1025.** What arrived at Asellby finished what the poison had started, so the blight had sat heavy on Asellby for years, so the fever arrived to a town already half-poisoned. The burial rolls at Asellby were kept and the cause was not, and the Dust Fever of 1025 is what the burial rolls are filed under.

**Year 1050.** The country around Glasholt Crag dried from the edges inward, so the poorest ground at Glasholt Crag was the first to be given up. The grain price at Glasholt Crag doubled and the wage did not. And the dry page is headed the Drought of 1050.

**Year 1075.** War came to Asellby. And the Crown and the magnates met at Asellby, which had no part in the quarrel. The fighting cost Asellby a third of its people and the constabulary stayed on: the page at Asellby is headed the War of 1075.

**Year 1075.** There was a funeral, and then Thare held the censer. Thare took the arrangement as found and left it as found. Nothing in Asellby was decided by the arrival of Thare.

**Year 1075.** The succession of 1075 was decided by force and recorded as a succession. And the objections did not stop, and Skorgrolvek held the chair anyway. The 12 regions were told afterwards that Skorgrolvek held it.

**Year 1075.** The water went out of the country around Asellby, and the price of water at Asellby was set by the people who had some. Two dry years is not a disaster in Asellby's ledgers and is one at Asellby, so the year went into the register as the Drought of 1075.

**Year 1075.** Something under Ramwill Reach turned out to be worth carrying: nothing about Ramwill Reach had changed except what was known about it. The money reached Ramwill Reach before the assessors, which is the only sequence that ever helps a town; the clerks called it the Ramwill Reach Find without saying what it was.

**Year 1075.** The Temple raised Vienne where the harm had been, so nothing official had come to Asellby until the faith did. Asellby wanted a constabulary and received a shrine.

**Year 1100.** The throne passed quietly to Bravere; Bravere inherited the arrangement whole and changed none of it. The Sovereign changes and the arrangement does not, and Bravere inherits it entire.

**Year 1100.** In 1100 the fighting stopped and the writing started, at Asellby. And the magnates ceded nothing but their claim and kept their ledger, which held little, which is what the Crown had come to Asellby for. The paper at Asellby records a balance of ledgers and calls it a peace, so the clerks filed the fair copy as the Peace of Asellby.

**Year 1100.** Ramwill Reach found something and did not have to build it: the assessment at Ramwill Reach caught up within three years. The road to Ramwill Reach was repaired inside a year, which fifty years of petitions had not managed. The Ramwill Reach Find is the heading and the contents are left vague.

**Year 1125.** The money that had built Asellby was withdrawn from it. And the wire to Asellby was never taken up, and nothing was ever sent along it again. The charter for Asellby is still on file and refers to nothing standing.

**Year 1125.** The water came through Ramwill Reach in 1125 and stayed a fortnight. Nobody at Ramwill Reach was compensated and nobody had expected to be. The count of the dead at Ramwill Reach is an estimate and always was, so the water year is entered as the Ramwill Reach Flood.

**Year 1125.** A storm out of the open water found Asellby in 1125. The shipping at Asellby was lost at anchor. What Asellby lost was a season, and a season is what it lives on. The Great Storm of 1125 is the name that stuck.

**Year 1150.** The Osmark Combine arrived at Ramwill Reach Harbor with money instead of soldiers. The yards at Ramwill Reach kept every worker and changed every owner. The ledger that governs Ramwill Reach is kept where nobody in Ramwill Reach can read it.

**Year 1150.** The calls at Hamwick Harbor became regular in 1150, so an invitation is cheaper than a fleet, and Hamwick Harbor is being invited. Asellby saw the envoys and recorded nothing.

**Year 1150.** Hamwick Harbor was the landing, and 4 regions followed it. The assessment moved to the water at Hamwick Harbor and stayed there. The 4 occupied regions are the best-connected and worst-paid ground in the realm. And it is entered as the Landing at Hamwick in a language Hamwick does not use.

**Year 1150.** No claim was pressed against Vathrek, so Vathrek took the chair. And the peaceful ones are the short entries, and Vathrek got a short entry. The title at Asellby changed to Vathrek and the arrangement did not.

**Year 1150.** The water threw a storm at Asellby and held it there, and what Asellby lost was a season, and a season is what most of it lives on. Nothing was rebuilt at Asellby that did not earn its rebuilding: the clerks entered it as the Great Storm of 1150.

**Year 1150.** A find was made at Ramwill Reach that nobody had budgeted for; for one generation Ramwill Reach was worth going to. The people who had already left Ramwill Reach did not come back, so the Ramwill Reach Find is how it is filed, without a description.

**Year 1175.** Asellby chartered what the magnates had already begun at Hamwick, and the copper reached Hamwick on the strength of an assay. Nothing was asked of Hamwick and nothing was offered to it either.

**Year 1175.** The throne stood empty for a season after the Sovereign died, and by the end of it Lasellmoor held the throne and the treasury held less. Neither the ledger nor the map at Asellby records that Lasellmoor arrived.

**Year 1175.** The water table under Asellby dropped below the wells: the wells that had been common at Asellby stopped being common. The water that was left at Asellby went where water always goes, which is to whoever owns the channel, and the Drought of 1175 is the entry, and it is a short one.

**Year 1200.** The old Hierarch died, and the succession was contested. And the gates went unwatched for as long as it lasted, and the rivals of Asellby noticed. The ledger at Asellby did not notice that a Hierarch had become Corisonne.

**Year 1200.** The river took the bottom of Ramwill Reach. The survey had marked it and the leases at Ramwill Reach were signed anyway. The leases at Ramwill Reach still run to the water: it is the Ramwill Reach Flood in the register and something shorter in Ramwill Reach.

**Year 1200.** The wind held onto the Asellby shore and would not let go of it. And the exposure that makes Asellby a harbour is the exposure that emptied it. The exposure that makes a harbour is the exposure that empties it, at Asellby as everywhere. The Great Storm of 1200 is the year in every account that survives.

**Year 1225.** The luck at Ramwill Reach turned upward, and nobody had predicted it, so what was found at Ramwill Reach outvalued everything built there. What was found at Ramwill Reach was worth more than everything anyone had built there; the register calls it the Ramwill Reach Find.

**Year 1250.** When the First Magnate died the question of the chair was open, and it stayed open: nothing was governed for the length of it, and the 12 regions noticed. Kravzeth inherits a First Magnate's ledger and none of a First Magnate's choices.

**Year 1250.** The wells at Asellby were reading low by midsummer and dry by the next, and the ground furthest from the channels went first, as it does everywhere around Asellby. Nothing at Asellby was rationed, which is a decision even unmade, so the scribes titled the dry page the Drought of 1250.

**Year 1250.** The water rose at Ramwill Reach in 1250 and took a fortnight to leave. What the river took at Ramwill Reach it took from the people who could not afford to build higher. The survey had the Ramwill Reach floodline on it and the leases ignored it, so it is filed as the Ramwill Reach Flood, with the count left blank.

**Year 1250.** The ground at Ramwill Reach was suddenly worth the trouble, so the wagons came to Ramwill Reach before anyone official did. The people who left Ramwill Reach before it came back did not come back; the Ramwill Reach Find is the entry and the entry says nothing else.

Bynames settled on the places the years used hardest: Fellton the Shadowed, Dravzek Crag the Lofted, Khekton the Hollow, Ramwill Reach the Yoked, Glasholt Crag the Hollow, Asellby the Hollow, Brackby the Yoked, Serrvarnuk Bastion the Hollow, Hamwick the Yoked, High Marton the Lofted and Calvermere the Yoked, though those 11 bynames were granted by no charter and can be lifted by no decree, which makes them the plainest record in this document.

## The State of the Realm, Year 1250

By the last epoch, of 9 settled regions, 1 gained, 7 ended where the founding left them and 1 did not survive the years. And the maps still name all 3 deadholds, but the roads have stopped going there.

Khekton, Glasholt Crag and Serrvarnuk Bastion are deadholds now: 3 holdings stand where towns used to.

Ramwill Reach rose further than any other place in the record: what rose was the value of the ground, and Ramwill Reach happened to be standing on it.

The realm divides 6 to the Crown, 3 to the Temple and 1 to the magnates, and 1 is contested between them, and 1 answers to no one at all.

4 gates stand in the realm, held 0 Crown, 2 Temple, 0 magnate; a levy at a gate is not a price, because a price implies a choice, and the 4 offer none.

What the gates collected went mostly to the Temple. And the loop is closed: gates pay for gates, and the Crown and the Temple are the pair the capital watches.

The owners' row is 3.6 in the hundred and holds 43.9 in the hundred: 20.8 times better is not a figure of speech, it is the ratio, and at Ramwill Reach it is worse.

Over all of it stands the Dominion, which holds 4 regions from its foothold at Hamwick and calls the arrangement trade, though the Crown still reigns, and it no longer rules the 4 regions the Dominion holds, and the two are not the same thing.

3 settlements end the record unwired, and a projection drew that line and 3 settlements fell the wrong side of it.

## What the Record Shows

The record closes with what the numbers say about 12 regions. This world got more unequal and poorer at the bottom: the gap ran 0.29 at the founding and 0.42 at the close; the founding roll set 0.29 and the centuries did the rest. 10 epochs and no hinge at all: neither reform nor rising came. Read as periods rather than years, it runs the Age of the Gates (1000–1050, gini 0.39 → 0.31), the Age of Tariffs (1075–1250, gini 0.30 → 0.42). Nothing chose those boundaries but the turns of the gini itself, 0.29 to 0.42. Counted over inhabited towns only, the bottom fifth carries 0.4× the top fifth's blight. At 0.4× it is the wealthy end carrying the heavier load, which these rules permit and do not require. Wealth and blight both cluster, at 0.085 and 0.484, where no structure would return -0.091, and treat it as a property of the generated map: smoothing kernels put correlation into the terrain before the first town was placed (p 0.130 / 0.005). At 8 regions the crossing is held by someone the region did not pick, and the tariff is paid anyway. Geology chose the 3 narrow places and whoever held them at the founding still does.
