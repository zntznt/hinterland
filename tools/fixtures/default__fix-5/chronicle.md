# A Chronicle of the Hinterland

*Set down at Umbereness in 1250, under Comfre, concerning the world called "fix-5".*

## The Founding, Year 1000

A realm of 12 regions, kept from Umbereness, and the ground decided most of this before anyone arrived, and the northwest wind decided the rest.

From 3927 at the median to 6671 at the head, and not a line of it planned, so advantage compounds, and over 12 regions it compounded into this spread.

The refining is done at Umbereness and consumed where it is made: the Temple holds sacred ground at Umbrine (by Grendhul), out where the aetherstone lies and the capital's authority is weak.

Between Khelth and Umbereness the Browmere Lane carries what will pay the lift. A Browmere lane is cut where the walk is worst and the freight is richest, and the fare decides who gets the saving.

The wall is Narzek Teeth, Dharzek Teeth and Zolzar Teeth, and everything that crosses it crosses at Brok Stair, Kordrok Stair and Krak Steps, and 8 regions sit in the mountains' shadow, cut off from Umbereness by the wall, and it costs them.

The sea lies to the east, and the charts call it Markhor Sea. The realm's gate is Umbereness Harbor: everything the mines raise and the aetherworks refine leaves through them, and whoever holds the quay collects the tariff.

From the high ground at Oxenby the Mermook Rill falls through 2 regions and out of the realm: the towns drink from it in order, so Oxenby gets it clean and Gleighby at the mouth gets whatever every town and aetherworks above has put in it.

The realm is built over an older one, and the delve called Khalvek gapes in the old workings by Grendhul, Velithiene was buried by Hazelmoord Cairn before the founding and has not been disturbed and by Gharton the deadhold Kyrielune keeps its walls and its poison: delvers work the Neyholt road every season because it pays when nothing else does, not all of them come back, and what they carry out is sold off the books.

Gleighby Tower stand where neither the grid nor the law arrived, and an apostate keeps them, so there is exactly one in 12 regions, and the Temple calls it heresy anyway.

The water is bridged at Gleighby Bridge and Oxenby Bridge. And the 2 spans were built by the realm and are held by whoever got there first.

## The Years

The record does not run flat: it falls into 4 ages, **the Quiet Years** (1000–1025), when the spread closed up, **the Gated Age** (1050–1075), when the gap held, **the Restoration** (1100–1150), when the spread closed up and **the Age of Tariffs** (1175–1250), in which the gap got wider. And the 4 names were not granted by anybody; they are what the wealth, the tariffs and the emptied towns add up to.

**Year 1025.** A quarrel between the Verrant Hegemony and the Metropole became the ruin of Umbereness. The wealth the trade had brought to Umbereness was gone, and the town had no say in any of it. The second fortune at Umbereness was made on trade and unmade the same way.

**Year 1025.** The Umbereness gate went to the magnates on a claim nobody contested. And the notice went up at Umbereness and the queue formed under it the same week. The 12 regions gained no road that year and lost a free crossing.

**Year 1050.** The rains failed over Larroor, and failed again. The price of water at Larroor was set by whoever still had some. Two dry years is not a disaster in Umbereness's ledgers and is one at Larroor. And the clerks headed the year the Drought of 1050.

**Year 1075.** The chair passed quietly to Drumdek: the treasury at Umbereness paid for a funeral and for nothing else. Nothing about the arrangement over 12 regions turned on whether the name was Drumdek.

**Year 1100.** The lode under Umbereness was mined to nothing, and the aetherworks stopped with it. Everyone at Umbereness who could leave left, in the order their savings allowed. What Umbereness was built for lasted one lode.

**Year 1100.** The Crown put a post on the Umbereness road and called it a gate; the tariff post at Umbereness went up before the claim was answered. One more of the 12 regions now pays a stranger to leave itself.

**Year 1100.** An apostate raised a tower at Gleighby, out where no writ runs and the grid never came: the writ that would have stopped it does not reach Gleighby, which is why it was raised there. Nothing legal was on offer at Gleighby and this was.

**Year 1100.** Whatever the old world left under Grendhul broke loose: the records are thin and frightened, and the blighted ring around Grendhul kept everyone honest about that much. The seal on the Grendhul site is the whole of the response.

**Year 1100.** The Crown Granary passed at Umbereness after years of not passing, so it is the first measure in the Umbereness register that moves coin downhill on purpose. Umbereness conceded the arithmetic, not the argument.

**Year 1100.** Grendhul broke the constabulary line and kept the town, so no tariff has been collected at Grendhul since. The skilled workers followed the money out of Grendhul and the free town starved, which is the other thing that happens. The date is kept at Grendhul as the Grendhul Rising.

**Year 1125.** Umbereness Harbor was taken without a shot in 1125, so the assessment moved to the water at Umbereness Harbor and stayed there. Those 2 regions are the best-connected and worst-paid ground in the realm. And the Dominion's own registers file it as the Landing at Umbereness.

**Year 1125.** The crossing at Grendhul belonged to the Temple by the end of the year: no blood is recorded, the claim was made on paper, and the tariff at Grendhul kept it. Nobody who crosses at Grendhul was asked, and nobody has a second road.

**Year 1125.** Grendhul became a destination in 1125 without doing anything; the favour was not asked for and the tariff on it was collected all the same at Grendhul. The innkeepers at Grendhul did better out of it than the priests did; it stands in the Temple register as the Rise of Grendhul.

**Year 1150.** Aetherworks rose at Umbereness on a fresh charter; the capital sealed the charter, and the trunk line reached Umbereness within the season. The towns the trunk line passed on the way to Umbereness are not named in the charter.

**Year 1150.** Armies met at Hazelmoord Cairn in 1150; the two powers fighting there were the Crown and the Temple, and Hazelmoord Cairn was just where they met. Hazelmoord Cairn was poorer by a quarter and emptier by a third when it stopped: it survives as the Hazelmoord Cairn War in both sides' copies.

**Year 1150.** The Sovereign died in 1150 and the succession went as written, to Comfre; nobody in the 12 regions was consulted, and nobody expected to be. Nothing in Umbereness was decided by the arrival of Comfre.

**Year 1150.** Rumth succeeded the old First Magnate and nobody contested it. What Rumth inherited was the arrangement and not the choosing of it. The 12 regions kept paying through it, to Rumth instead.

**Year 1150.** A plague settled on Cravergate: the blight had sat heavy on Cravergate for years, so the fever arrived to a town already half-poisoned. The burial rolls at Cravergate were kept and the cause was not, so the scribes wrote it down as the Long Cough of 1150 and moved on.

**Year 1150.** This measure did not come from Umbereness, it came from the creditors of Umbereness, so the books of 12 regions were balanced on whoever could least object. The official term is structural adjustment, and the towns of the 12 regions called it a decree written in another capital.

**Year 1175.** The Hierarch died in 1175, and the transfer of the censer took a fortnight. The peaceful ones are the short entries, and Galielaine got a short entry. What a Hierarch inherits is a ledger, and the ledger at Umbereness did not change hands with Galielaine.

**Year 1175.** Hazelmoord Cairn was chosen for the table because the fighting had stopped there; the Crown set it down as the Temple ceded a gate and paid 3 in tribute out of its ledger. What was agreed at Hazelmoord Cairn had been decided before anyone sat down, and the archive at Umbereness files it as the Peace of Hazelmoord Cairn.

**Year 1175.** The streams that feed Larroor ran to gravel. And grazing around Larroor failed a season before the grain did. The herds around Larroor were sold at whatever was offered. And it survives as the Drought of 1175, three lines long.

**Year 1175.** The ground gave under Hazelmoord Cairn in 1175. And nothing about it was a surprise to anyone who had read the survey of Hazelmoord Cairn. The pass above Hazelmoord Cairn was the cheap route, and it was days before it was a route again, and the entry at Umbereness reads the Hazelmoord Cairn Quake and runs three lines.

**Year 1175.** Umbereness turned up something the surveys had missed: the road to Umbereness was mended by people who wanted to use it. The people who had already left Umbereness did not come back. And the Umbereness Find is the heading and the contents are left vague.

**Year 1200.** The Temple arrived at Grendhul with a document and left with a gate. The people who used the Grendhul crossing found the fee waiting the next market day. The 12 regions gained no road that year and lost a free one.

**Year 1200.** What the surveys had walked past at Umbereness was found by accident; for a while Umbereness was somewhere to go rather than somewhere to leave. The people who left Umbereness before it came back did not come back; the Umbereness Find is how it is filed, without a description.

**Year 1225.** Larroor watched its ground go pale and then go hard: the fields nearest the channels held, and everything past them at Larroor did not. The grain price at Larroor doubled and the wage did not, so the scribes titled the dry page the Drought of 1225.

**Year 1250.** There was a funeral, and then Ilmarienne held the censer, so no gate changed hands over it, which the clerks at Umbereness noted as unusual. The Hierarch changes and the arrangement does not, and Ilmarienne inherits it entire.

**Year 1250.** The storm found Umbereness first, as the storms do. The wharves at Umbereness were rebuilt within the year, and the houses behind them were not. The tariff at Umbereness was collected that year on nothing: the clerks entered it as the Great Storm of 1250.

**Year 1250.** Umbereness found something the surveys had walked past, and people came back to ground at Umbereness they had been leaving. The money reached Umbereness before the assessors, which is the only sequence that ever helps a town. The register calls it the Umbereness Find.

The years leave names behind them, and the realm now speaks of Cravergate the Ashen, Khelth the Lofted, Hazelmoord Cairn the Hollow, Umbereness the Yoked, Larroor the Yoked, Neyholt the Shadowed, Pellwick the Open, Grendhul the Famished, Gharton the Hollow and Gleighby the Lofted, though those 10 bynames were granted by no charter and can be lifted by no decree, which makes them the plainest record in this document.

## The State of the Realm, Year 1250

By the last epoch, of 9 settled regions, 8 held and 1 did not survive the years: nobody struck those 3 deadholds from the survey, and the traffic simply stopped.

3 holdings stand where a town was and is not.

Ground given up for dead was taken again at Gharton: nobody returned to it out of sentiment, and the name on the Umbereness charts shows it.

What the three powers hold is 8, 1 and 0. And 1 regions recognises no authority whatever, and 2 are claimed by more than one.

The 6 gates are held 1 Crown, 1 Temple, 0 magnate, and 2 stand untolled, and every levy on the list is paid by people who did not choose the road.

The Crown hold the coin, and the coin holds the next gate, so what the gates earn buys more gates, and of the powers the Crown and the Temple are the pair worth watching.

One name covers two peoples in every town, and 4 in the hundred of them hold 58 in the hundred of the coin. A regional map sees towns and not rows, and misses 20 parts in a hundred of the spread doing so.

2 regions answer across the water, from the foothold at Umbereness, and those 2 regions keep the smallest share of what they make and carry the best wires in the realm, both for the same reason.

4 settlements end the years exactly as dark as they began, so the line was drawn in Umbereness and fell where it fell, with 4 on the wrong side.

## What the Record Shows

What 12 regions come to, in figures. This world got more unequal, from 0.43 at the founding up to 0.54, and the same 12 regions carried both readings. The hinge is the Crown Granary in 1100. By the gini's own turns the record is 4 ages: the Quiet Years (1000–1025, gini 0.53 → 0.40), the Gated Age (1050–1075, gini 0.38 → 0.40), the Restoration (1100–1150, gini 0.48 → 0.40), the Age of Tariffs (1175–1250, gini 0.50 → 0.61): nothing chose those boundaries but the turns of the gini itself, 0.43 to 0.54. The poorest fifth of this realm's towns carries 1.4× the blight of its richest fifth, and across 12 regions the ground took it wherever the ground was cheapest. Clustering is computed from this world's own map: Moran's I puts wealth at 0.051 and blight at 0.000, against -0.091 expected under no structure; neighbouring cells resemble each other by construction here, so treat the figure as a property of the generated map (p 0.010 / 0.245). Behind the Narzek wall a median settlement takes 33% less than the open country at the same remove from the capital. The capital at Umbereness is the same walk away for both. Gleighby stands at the river mouth and takes 5 points of other towns' poison with the water: being last on 12 regions' river is not a policy anyone adopted. 8 regions hand a tariff to gate-holders they had no part in choosing. 2 of 6 crossings have fallen past their upkeep, which raises what the passage costs again. The Narzek takes the rain: 40 on the windward side, 8 in the lee, and a 40-to-8 split in the rain is upstream of every settlement decision that followed. Pellwick and Khelth stand the same distance from the capital, one in the open and one behind the wall. Wealth 15 against 9 and burden 48.6 against 50, with the rock as the only variable. Ranked by size, the towns lie on a line: α 1.55 over the system, α 0.15 in the big-town tail. The law was written in at the founding by proportional growth, so it is not evidence of anything; the slope is, at α 0.15 and fit 0.89. Flight would help the walled country most: 49.6% off its road to the capital against 0% off the open country's, and the fare is the filter: the lane saves 49.6% for whoever can board, and the walk is what the rest keep.

10 epochs of it, 35 figures, and no hand on the scale: every value recomputes from the exported columns.
