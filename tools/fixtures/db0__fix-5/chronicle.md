# A Chronicle of the Hinterland

*Set down at Umbereness in 1250, under Comfre, concerning the world called "fix-5".*

## The Founding, Year 1000

A realm of 12 regions, kept from Umbereness, and the ground decided most of this before anyone arrived, and the northwest wind decided the rest.

From 3927 at the median to 6671 at the head, and not a line of it planned, so advantage compounds, and over 12 regions it compounded into this spread.

The refining is done at Umbereness and consumed where it is made: the Temple holds sacred ground at Umbrine (by Grendhul), out where the aetherstone lies and the capital's authority is weak.

Between Khelth and Umbereness the Browmere Lane carries what will pay the lift. A Browmere lane is cut where the walk is worst and the freight is richest, and the fare decides who gets the saving.

The wall is Narzek Teeth, Dharzek Teeth and Zolzar Teeth, and everything that crosses it crosses at Brok Stair, Kordrok Stair and Krak Steps, and 8 regions sit in the mountains' shadow, cut off from Umbereness by the wall, and it costs them.

The sea lies to the east, and the charts call it Markhor Sea. The realm's gate is Umbereness Harbor: everything the mines raise and the aetherworks refine leaves through them, and whoever holds the quay collects the tariff.

From the high ground at Oxenby the Mermook Rill falls through 2 regions and out of the realm: the towns drink from it in order, so Oxenby gets it clean and Gleighby at the mouth gets whatever every town and aetherworks above has put in it.

The realm is built over an older one, and the delve called Khalvek gapes in the old workings by Grendhul, Velithiene was buried by Hazelmoord Cairn before the founding and has not been disturbed and by Gharton the deadhold Kyrielune keeps its walls and its poison: delvers work the Gharton road every season because it pays when nothing else does, not all of them come back, and what they carry out is sold off the books.

Cravergate Tower and Gleighby Tower stand where neither the grid nor the law arrived, and apostates keep them, so those 2 towers are illegal and the wire's absence across 12 regions is not.

The water is bridged at Gleighby Bridge and Oxenby Bridge. And the 2 spans were built by the realm and are held by whoever got there first.

## The Years

The record does not run flat: it falls into 4 ages, **the Quiet Years** (1000–1025), when the spread closed up, **the Gated Age** (1050–1075), when the gap held, **the Restoration** (1100–1150), when the spread closed up and **the Age of Tariffs** (1175–1250), in which the gap got wider. And the 4 names were not granted by anybody; they are what the wealth, the tariffs and the emptied towns add up to.

**Year 1025.** A quarrel between the Verrant Hegemony and the Metropole became the ruin of Umbereness. The wealth the trade had brought to Umbereness was gone, and the town had no say in any of it. The second fortune at Umbereness was made on trade and unmade the same way.

**Year 1025.** The Umbereness gate went to the magnates on a claim nobody contested. And the notice went up at Umbereness and the queue formed under it the same week. The 12 regions gained no road that year and lost a free crossing.

**Year 1050.** The rains failed over Larroor, and failed again. The price of water at Larroor was set by whoever still had some. Two dry years is not a disaster in Umbereness's ledgers and is one at Larroor. And the clerks headed the year the Drought of 1050.

**Year 1075.** The chair passed quietly to Drumdek: the treasury at Umbereness paid for a funeral and for nothing else. Nothing about the arrangement over 12 regions turned on whether the name was Drumdek.

**Year 1100.** The seam under Umbereness ran out and took the yards with it, and the charter for Umbereness is still on file at Umbereness and refers to nothing. What Umbereness was built for lasted one lode.

**Year 1100.** The Crown pressed its claim on the crossing at Umbereness. A gate is a quay, a span or a pass, and whoever holds one holds a line of people who cannot go around Umbereness. The crossing at Umbereness cost nothing to build and pays forever.

**Year 1100.** Somebody set up at Gleighby where the grid had never come, and the wire had refused Gleighby and this had not. The writ that would have stopped it never reached Gleighby in the first place.

**Year 1100.** Something of the old world stirred and broke in the ground by Grendhul. The Temple sealed the site and said little, and the scar it burned into the country by Grendhul is still there to read. The seal on the Grendhul site is the whole of the response.

**Year 1100.** The Crown Granary passed at Umbereness after years of not passing; it taxes the fat years to buy bread for the lean ones, and it is the first decree in the history of Umbereness to move coin downhill. Umbereness had heard the argument for years and acted on the arithmetic.

**Year 1100.** The rising at Grendhul held, and no tariff has been collected at Grendhul since. The injustice had been real and so was the freedom, and neither one fed anybody in Grendhul, so the date survives as the Grendhul Rising, unofficially.

**Year 1125.** The Dominion took Umbereness Harbor and did not have to fight for it; the quays at Umbereness Harbor collect for a power across the sea now. Umbereness reigns over Umbereness Harbor and does not govern it. And the Dominion's registers head it the Landing at Umbereness.

**Year 1125.** The Temple decided the gate at Grendhul was worth having, so there was no fight worth recording, and the first tariff notice at Grendhul went up before the ink on the claim was dry. One more of the realm's 12 regions now pays to leave itself.

**Year 1125.** Grendhul became a destination in 1125 without doing anything, and nothing was built at Grendhul and everything was worth more. The innkeepers at Grendhul did better out of it than the priests did; the Rise of Grendhul is what the faithful call it, and they call it that still.

**Year 1150.** Aetherworks rose at Umbereness on a fresh charter; the capital sealed the charter, and the trunk line reached Umbereness within the season. The towns the trunk line passed on the way to Umbereness are not named in the charter.

**Year 1150.** Armies met at Hazelmoord Cairn in 1150; the two powers fighting there were the Crown and the Temple, and Hazelmoord Cairn was just where they met. Hazelmoord Cairn was poorer by a quarter and emptier by a third when it stopped: it survives as the Hazelmoord Cairn War in both sides' copies.

**Year 1150.** The Sovereign died in 1150 and the succession went as written, to Comfre; nobody in the 12 regions was consulted, and nobody expected to be. Nothing in Umbereness was decided by the arrival of Comfre.

**Year 1150.** Rumth succeeded the old First Magnate and nobody contested it. What Rumth inherited was the arrangement and not the choosing of it. The 12 regions kept paying through it, to Rumth instead.

**Year 1150.** The adjustment of 1150 was written in another capital and signed in Umbereness. And the books of 12 regions were balanced on whoever could least object. What Umbereness could not be argued into it was lent into.

**Year 1175.** The censer passed to Galielaine in 1175, exactly as the charter said it would, so the ledgers at Umbereness carried on without a correction. Umbereness kept the date and the name Galielaine and dropped everything else.

**Year 1175.** In 1175 the fighting stopped and the writing started, at Hazelmoord Cairn; the Temple ceded a gate and paid 3 in tribute out of its ledger, which is what the Crown had come to Hazelmoord Cairn for. The losing side signed at Hazelmoord Cairn because signing was cheaper than continuing. The copy at Umbereness is titled the Peace of Hazelmoord Cairn.

**Year 1175.** The water table under Larroor dropped below the wells: the wells that had been common at Larroor stopped being common. The grain price at Larroor doubled and the wage did not. And the year went into the register as the Drought of 1175.

**Year 1175.** The folding under Hazelmoord Cairn slipped, and nothing at Hazelmoord Cairn was insured, because nothing at Hazelmoord Cairn was insurable. The fold runs the length of these 12 regions and Hazelmoord Cairn sits on it; the Hazelmoord Cairn Quake is the entry, and the entry is short.

**Year 1175.** Something under Umbereness turned out to be worth carrying. And what was found at Umbereness was worth more than everything built there. The money reached Umbereness before the assessors, which is the only sequence that ever helps a town, so the register calls it the Umbereness Find.

**Year 1200.** A claim was filed on the Grendhul crossing and enforced the same season. Nothing about the crossing at Grendhul changed except who collected at it. Gates are the cheapest thing in the realm to take and the dearest to be under, at Grendhul as anywhere.

**Year 1200.** The ground at Umbereness was suddenly worth the trouble; for a while Umbereness was somewhere to go rather than somewhere to leave. For a generation Umbereness was somewhere to arrive at; the Umbereness Find is how it is filed, without a description.

**Year 1225.** The streams above Larroor were down to stones by 1225. And the shared wells at Larroor stopped being shared inside a season. The drought at Larroor was a shortage for some and a market for others. The Drought of 1225 is what the dry page is headed.

**Year 1250.** No claim was pressed against Ilmarienne, so Ilmarienne took the censer. Ilmarienne took the arrangement as found and left it as found. The title at Umbereness changed to Ilmarienne and the arrangement did not.

**Year 1250.** The wind held onto the Cravergate shore and would not let go of it. What was insured at Cravergate was replaced and what was not was not. The exposure that makes a harbour is the exposure that empties it, at Cravergate as everywhere. The clerks entered it as the Great Storm of 1250.

**Year 1250.** Umbereness found something the surveys had walked past; the road to Umbereness was mended by people who wanted to use it. The people who had already left Umbereness did not come back: the clerks called it the Umbereness Find without saying what it was.

The years leave names behind them, and the realm now speaks of Cravergate the Shadowed, Khelth the Lofted, Hazelmoord Cairn the Hollow, Umbereness the Yoked, Larroor the Yoked, Neyholt the Shadowed, Pellwick the Open, Grendhul the Famished, Gharton the Ashen and Gleighby the Lofted, though those 10 bynames were granted by no charter and can be lifted by no decree, which makes them the plainest record in this document.

## The State of the Realm, Year 1250

By the last epoch, of 10 settled regions, 9 held and 1 did not survive the years: nobody struck those 2 deadholds from the survey, and the traffic simply stopped.

2 holdings stand where a town was and is not.

Ground given up for dead was taken again at Gharton: nobody returned to it out of sentiment, and the name on the Umbereness charts shows it.

What the three powers hold is 8, 1 and 0. And 1 regions recognises no authority whatever, and 2 are claimed by more than one.

The 6 gates are held 1 Crown, 1 Temple, 0 magnate, and 2 stand untolled, and every levy on the list is paid by people who did not choose the road.

The Crown hold the coin, and the coin holds the next gate, so what the gates earn buys more gates, and of the powers the Crown and the Temple are the pair worth watching.

One name covers two peoples in every town, and 3.9 in the hundred of them hold 57.9 in the hundred of the coin. A regional map sees towns and not rows, and misses 19 parts in a hundred of the spread doing so.

2 regions answer across the water, from the foothold at Umbereness, and those 2 regions keep the smallest share of what they make and carry the best wires in the realm, both for the same reason.

4 settlements end the years exactly as dark as they began, so the line was drawn in Umbereness and fell where it fell, with 4 on the wrong side.

## What the Record Shows

What 12 regions come to, in figures. Extraction: the spread opened from 0.48 to 0.58 and the poorest tenth fell, and the same 12 regions carried both readings. The hinge is the Crown Granary in 1100. By the gini's own turns the record is 4 ages: the Quiet Years (1000–1025, gini 0.53 → 0.40), the Gated Age (1050–1075, gini 0.38 → 0.40), the Restoration (1100–1150, gini 0.48 → 0.41), the Age of Tariffs (1175–1250, gini 0.50 → 0.63): nothing chose those boundaries but the turns of the gini itself, 0.48 to 0.58. The poorest fifth of this realm's towns carries 0.5× the blight of its richest fifth, and at 0.5× it is the wealthy end carrying the heavier load, which these rules permit and do not require. Clustering is computed from this world's own map: Moran's I puts wealth at 0.049 and blight at -0.219, against -0.091 expected under no structure; neighbouring cells resemble each other by construction here, so treat the figure as a property of the generated map (p 0.010 / 0.220). Behind the Narzek wall a median settlement takes 33% less than the open country at the same remove from the capital. The capital at Umbereness is the same walk away for both. Gleighby stands at the river mouth and takes 6 points of other towns' poison with the water: being last on 12 regions' river is not a policy anyone adopted. 9 regions hand a tariff to gate-holders they had no part in choosing. 2 of 6 crossings have fallen past their upkeep, which raises what the passage costs again. The Narzek takes the rain: 40 on the windward side, 8 in the lee, and a 40-to-8 split in the rain is upstream of every settlement decision that followed. Pellwick and Khelth stand the same distance from the capital, one in the open and one behind the wall. Wealth 15 against 9 and burden 53.8 against 53.9, with the rock as the only variable. Ranked by size, the towns lie on a line: α 1.52 over the system, α 0.15 in the big-town tail. The law was written in at the founding by proportional growth, so it is not evidence of anything; the slope is, at α 0.15 and fit 0.91. Flight would help the walled country most: 50.2% off its road to the capital against 0% off the open country's, and the fare is the filter: the lane saves 50.2% for whoever can board, and the walk is what the rest keep.

10 epochs of it, 35 figures, and no hand on the scale: every value recomputes from the exported columns.

*Widened distance and a fallen floor, leaving ten regions further apart and worse off at the bottom, and the distance is fifty-eight in the hundred, against forty-eight when Umbereness was surveyed. Eight of the ten regions are poorer at the close than at the founding. And there is no more and no less to divide across ten regions than there was. None of Umbereness was steered, and all of it is chargeable to rules written down before the first settlement.*
