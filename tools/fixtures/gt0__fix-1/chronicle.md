# A Chronicle of the Hinterland

*A fair copy made at Brook in 1250 under Wornweld, of the world called "fix-1".*

## The Founding, Year 1000

12 regions, one capital at Brook, and this account of both, though the weather comes east and the rest came from underneath: the ore, the gradient, and what a wagon can climb.

At the founding the spread ran 2790 to 9029, and nobody set it. And nothing decided it but arithmetic run for centuries over 12 regions, where every town grew by a share of itself.

The refining is done at Brook and consumed where it is made; the Temple holds sacred ground at Galcyonne (by Cerandre Cairn), out where the aetherstone lies and the capital's authority is weak.

Lift-barges work the Eldhartholt Lane between Brook and Cerandre Cairn. The Eldhartholt was cut where the walk is worst and the cargo richest, which is the same place twice.

Zolzek Teeth, Belkrakvar Teeth, Zolgrak Teeth and Dhorn Teeth stand across the realm and the roads must find Zulzak Saddle and Drellok Pass to get over: 8 regions are on the far side of it, further from Brook in cost than in distance.

Open water lies to the east and north, and it is drawn as Comfre Waters: sailors keep well clear of the Orvallenne, where the sea turns on itself, and no quay was ever built within its reach.

The Crack Beck falls from Brokford and crosses 3 regions on its way out. What 3 regions put into it, Merelmere takes out of it.

The old world is still here: Zulgravdurn, a delve past Cerandre Cairn, has never been surveyed to the bottom, Elisith lies in a tomb out in the barrens past Brokhold and Skarremmolt, a delve past Drakby, has never been surveyed to the bottom, so what comes up the Brokhold road is sold before anyone official hears of it.

Viel Refuge stands above Vrakton on ground no authority granted. And the healing at Viel Refuge is free and the climb is the price, and nobody who takes it appears in any register.

Trek Camp by Cerandre Cairn were built by the people who use them and licensed by nobody: a bounty is the one wage in these 12 regions that asks for no charter.

The realm has one span, at Merelmere Bridge, and no other crossing. And the alternative to the single span is a ford, and across 12 regions the fords are counted in wagons rather than in tariffs.

## The Years

4 ages carry the years between them: **the Age of the Gates** (1000–1050), when the shape of the realm sat still, **the Age of Tariffs** (1075–1125), when the spread closed up, **the Hollow Years** (1150–1200), in which the gap got smaller and **the Age of the Crossings** (1225–1250), when the gap held. Nothing named those 4 periods but the shape of the record: the pile-ups, the levies, and the towns that went dark.

**Year 1025.** The charter at Drakby Harbor was signed rather than imposed. And the yards at Drakby kept every worker and changed every owner. Half of what the ground at Drakby yields is entered in a ledger nobody in the realm may read.

**Year 1025.** A quarrel elsewhere shut the sea to Drakby, so the quays at Drakby stood full and still for a year. Drakby discovered what a fortune built on somebody else's politics is worth.

**Year 1025.** What the founding surveys called Umberthold the first winter called uninhabitable; the survey that placed Umberthold read the water and the road and did not read the ground.

**Year 1025.** Dhozzurn appears in the founding survey and in no census after it: Dhozzurn is a name on a map and was never anything else.

**Year 1025.** The ground at Cerandre Cairn never carried anyone, whatever the founding rolls entered. The rolls of Brook carried it for a season and then stopped.

**Year 1050.** Drakby was under weather for three days and under water for one. The Drakby defences were built to the standard the ledgers would pay for. The quay at Drakby held because the quay had money spent on it, and the Great Storm of 1050 is the name that stuck.

**Year 1075.** The aetherworks at Brook closed its yards for good, and Brook kept its wires and lost its wages. The magnates who left Brook took the machinery and left the housing.

**Year 1075.** No claim was pressed against Velane, so Velane took the censer: nobody in the 12 regions was consulted, and nobody expected to be. Brook kept the date and the name Velane and dropped everything else.

**Year 1075.** There was a funeral, and then Volzar held the chair. The peaceful ones are the short entries, and Volzar got a short entry. The 12 regions were informed by notice that Volzar had the title.

**Year 1075.** Nothing fell on Gordness for two summers together: the price of water at Gordness was set by whoever still had some. The drought at Gordness was a shortage for some and a market for others. And it survives as the Drought of 1075, three lines long.

**Year 1075.** The sea rose against Drakby in 1075, and the roofs at Drakby went in the first night and the wharves in the second. What Drakby lost was a season, and a season is what it lives on. The clerks wrote the Great Storm of 1075 and the survivors agreed with them.

**Year 1100.** The throne stood empty for a season after the Sovereign died. Holdenmere held the throne at the end of it and holds it still. Across 12 regions the only thing that changed was a name, and the name is Holdenmere.

**Year 1100.** The storm found Drakby first, as the storms do, so the shipping at Drakby was lost at anchor. The tariff at Drakby was collected that year on nothing, and the year survives as the Great Storm of 1100.

**Year 1125.** New aetherworks fires were lit at Drakby in 1125. Everything Drakby got, it got because the ore was worth it. The copper to Drakby was laid faster than any road to Drakby ever was.

**Year 1125.** The magnates came for the narrow ground at Drakby. And everyone who goes through Drakby still goes through, and pays. The crossing at Drakby cost nothing to build and pays forever.

**Year 1150.** The sickness came to Vrakton and the blight had prepared the ground, and the road out of Vrakton was the only treatment on offer. Those who could leave Vrakton left, in the order their savings allowed, so the roads named it the Grey Breath of 1150.

**Year 1150.** The rising at Vrakton lasted a season: the constabulary arrived after the hangings at Vrakton. The Vrakton Rising is the date they keep, and they keep it without permission.

**Year 1175.** The roads bent back toward Cerandre Cairn. And the inns at Cerandre Cairn were full and the granaries followed. Favour is not a thing anyone at Cerandre Cairn can hold on to. And the Rise of Cerandre Cairn is what the faithful call the year.

**Year 1200.** Wornweld succeeded the old Sovereign and nobody contested it: the ledgers at Brook carried on without a correction. The 12 regions kept paying through it, to Wornweld instead.

**Year 1200.** The streams that feed Cerandre Cairn ran to gravel. And the ground furthest from the channels went first, as it does everywhere around Cerandre Cairn. Two dry years is not a disaster in Brook's ledgers and is one at Cerandre Cairn. The clerks headed the year the Drought of 1200.

**Year 1200.** Fortune turned at Dhozzurn: a lode, a lost road, a relic worth the carrying; what was found at Dhozzurn was worth more than everything built there. The road to Dhozzurn was repaired in a year that fifty petitions had not moved. The clerks called it the Dhozzurn Find without saying what it was.

**Year 1225.** The quarrel over the censer began at a funeral. And Tessethe came out of it holding the censer, which is all this record will commit to. The Hierarch changes and the arrangement does not, and Tessethe inherits it entire.

**Year 1225.** The streams above Cerandre Cairn were down to stones by 1225; the fields nearest the channels held, and everything past them at Cerandre Cairn did not. What Cerandre Cairn lost was the margin it had never been allowed to build, so the Drought of 1225 is what the dry page is headed.

**Year 1225.** The weather turned on Brokhold in 1225 and stayed turned; the crews at Brokhold were not insured and the hulls were. The exposure that makes a harbour is the exposure that empties it, at Brokhold as everywhere. The clerks entered it as the Great Storm of 1225.

**Year 1225.** A find was made at Dhozzurn that nobody had budgeted for, and the road to Dhozzurn was mended by people who wanted to use it. Nothing about Dhozzurn had changed except what was known about it; the register calls it the Dhozzurn Find.

**Year 1225.** What had been quiet at Cerandre Cairn was loud again inside a season. The innkeepers at Cerandre Cairn did better out of it than the priests, and said so quietly. The Temple's share of what came to Cerandre Cairn was taken first; the faithful keep the year as the Rise of Cerandre Cairn.

**Year 1250.** Brazzul took the chair in 1250 and the old First Magnate was buried first. And the entry at Brook runs to one line, and that is generous. The 12 regions were told afterwards that Brazzul held it.

Drakby the Hollow, Vrakton the Ashen, Brokhold the Shadowed, Brokford the Shadowed, Umberthold the Hollow, Brook the Steadfast, Dhozzurn the Hollow, Krukgard the Shadowed, Cerandre Cairn the Hollow, Gordness the Lofted and Mallby the Steadfast are what the years left on the map; the people kept all 11 names on their own, without permission and without a register.

## The State of the Realm, Year 1250

By the last epoch, of 9 settled regions, 4 ended where the founding left them, 3 went down and 2 failed: the roads to those 3 deadholds were never closed, they were simply stopped being taken.

Of the ground that was settled at the founding, 3 holdings stand empty at the close.

2 places have been resettled: Dhozzurn and Cerandre Cairn. And the 2 names are not the old names, because the people who came back were not the people who left.

Dhozzurn rose further than any other place in the record, so where Dhozzurn stood mattered more than anything Dhozzurn decided.

Of the 12 regions, 7 answer to the Crown, 1 to the Temple and 1 to the magnates, so 3 answer to nobody and 0 answer to more than one, which are different problems.

The count of gates is 4, divided 1 to 0 to 1. And 2 of them take nothing, and the rest are paid by whoever had no other way through.

What the gates collected went mostly to the Crown. More gates mean more coin, which buys more gates, and of the powers the Crown and the Temple stand nearest to blows.

Counted by row rather than by town, 3.9 in every hundred hold 40.6 of every hundred coins, and the ratio is 16.9 to one, owner to labour, and at Brook it reaches 55 coins in the hundred.

Every settlement in the 12 regions is wired at the close. And that is written here plainly, across all 12 regions, so a later reader can check whether it lasted.

## What the Record Shows

What 12 regions come to, in figures. Nothing moved the shape of this world and the floor fell under it, 0.31 to 0.28, and the founding rolled 0.31 and 10 epochs of compounding did the rest. It turned on the revolt of 1150, and the rising was put down. Cut at the turns, the record gives the Age of the Gates (1000–1050, gini 0.39 → 0.40), the Age of Tariffs (1075–1125, gini 0.40 → 0.36), the Hollow Years (1150–1200, gini 0.37 → 0.28), the Age of the Crossings (1225–1250, gini 0.29 → 0.28); the 4 cut points are the series' own, not an editor's. Counted over inhabited towns only, the bottom fifth carries 1.8× the top fifth's blight: across 12 regions the ground took it wherever the ground was cheapest. Clustering is computed from this world's own map: Moran's I puts wealth at -0.108 and blight at -0.338, against -0.091 expected under no structure. Treat it as a property of the generated map: smoothing kernels put correlation into the terrain before the first town was placed (p 0.505 / 0.010). The last town on the water is Merelmere, and it carries 12 points of blight from upstream. Nobody in Merelmere chose to be last in the queue. 9 regions are charged at crossings they do not own, though the drag those tariffs put on trade across the realm reads 8.9. One side of the Zolzek gets 37 and the other 29: the wind over these 12 regions was set before the first settlement and has not been asked since. Mallby and Brokhold stand the same distance from the capital, one in the open and one behind the wall, and so Mallby returns wealth 14, market 75, burden 14.5; Brokhold returns 7, 39, 57.1, and the difference is the mountain. The size distribution is a rank-size hierarchy, fitted at α 1.79 overall and α 0.82 in the tail, though a size law is guaranteed here before any economy runs, which is why the claim is about steepness (α 0.82, fit 0.95) and not about shape. 1 coast is held by concession rather than by conquest; poorer than the median it sits in, 0 against 14, and still sending 50% of its yield to the Aurean League. 38.8% off the walled road, 0% off the open one: the sky is worth most where the ground is worst. The fare sorts who gets the 38.8%, and it is not the country that needs it.

There is no villain in this record and it happened anyway; every one of the 36 figures recomputes from the exported columns.

*The distance across nine regions held steady while the ground beneath it fell, which is a decline no spread can show. And the spread across nine regions sits at twenty-eight in the hundred. The bottom tenth of Brook reads four where it read five, though Brook gained eleven parts in the hundred across the run. The arithmetic of Brook is not in dispute, and the reading of it is offered as a reading.*
