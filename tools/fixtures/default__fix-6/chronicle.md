# A Chronicle of the Hinterland

*Asellby keeps this record of the world called "fix-6", closed in 1250 while Lasellmoor reigned.*

## The Founding, Year 1000

12 regions, one capital at Asellby, and this account of both, and the weather comes west and the rest came from underneath: the ore, the gradient, and what a wagon can climb.

At the founding the biggest town held 14917 and the middling one 2395: what a town already had is what it got more of, which is how 14917 pulled that far clear.

The refining is done at Asellby and consumed where it is made; 4 settlements started off the grid: reachable by road, but with no power line, because the ledgers said wiring them would not pay.

Between Dravzek Crag and Asellby the Merell Lane carries what will pay the lift. You pay at the aerie to board the Merell, so the sky belongs to whoever can afford the fare while the road belongs to everyone.

Dhulzar Teeth and Drennak Teeth stand across the realm and the roads must find Vrak Stair to get over; 5 regions are on the far side of it, further from Asellby in cost than in distance.

Open water lies to the east, and it is drawn as the Mermook Main. And the realm's gate is Hamwick Harbor: everything the mines raise and the aetherworks refine leaves through them, and whoever holds the quay collects the tariff.

2 regions take their water from the Briar Rill, which starts above Dravzek Crag, though the towns drink from it in order, so Dravzek Crag gets it clean and Ramwill Reach at the mouth gets whatever every town and aetherworks above has put in it.

The realm is built over an older one, and Kalgrek, a delve past Dravzek Crag, has never been surveyed to the bottom, the tomb of Nivelianthe keeps its silence in the barrens by Serrvarnuk Bastion and the old workings by Ramwill Reach are open at Gnakkelkrak and nobody has closed them: delvers work the Ramwill Reach road every season because it pays when nothing else does, not all of them come back, and what they carry out is sold off the books.

Asten Quay sits past the last stone by Brackby, on no charter and no map the capital keeps. And Asten Quay exists because the realm's own rules made it profitable, which is the only reason anything exists here.

The climb above Serrvarnuk Bastion ends at Ilyse Refuge, which was sanctioned by nobody, so nobody healed at Ilyse Refuge appears in any count the realm keeps.

The bounty country keeps Skaldek Camp by Dravzek Crag and keeps no records. And the stands thin the predation on that ground, the trophies are fenced where nothing is taxed, and for the poorest of the realm's 12 regions the bounty is the one rung of a ladder nobody built.

Ramwill Reach Bridge and Dravzek Crag Bridge carry every crossing in the realm, though the 2 spans were built by the realm and are held by whoever got there first.

## The Years

3 ages carry the years between them: **the Age of the Gates** (1000–1050), when the gap narrowed, **the Age of Tariffs** (1075–1100), when what separated the top from the middle grew and **the Still Years** (1125–1250), in which the gap did not move. The 3 boundaries are where the wealth turned, and nothing else marks them.

**Year 1025.** The sea closed on Hamwick in 1025, and the second fortune at Hamwick was made on trade and unmade the same way. Nothing at Hamwick was destroyed and everything at Hamwick stopped.

**Year 1050.** The country around Glasholt Crag dried from the edges inward; the country the water had barely reached went to dust first, as it always does around Glasholt Crag. Nothing at Glasholt Crag was rationed, which is a decision even unmade. And the scribes titled the dry page the Drought of 1050.

**Year 1075.** The fighting reached Asellby in 1075: the quarrel belonged to the Crown and the magnates and the ground belonged to Asellby. The capital lost a third of its people and a quarter of what it had. And the fair copy at Asellby is headed the Asellby War.

**Year 1075.** The Hierarch was buried and Thare was installed in the same month. And what changed at Asellby was a name at the head of the page. What Thare inherits across 12 regions is a ledger, and it did not move.

**Year 1075.** The First Magnate died without settling who came next. The register at Asellby names the survivor and not the dispute. Nothing in Asellby was decided by the arrival of Skorgrolvek.

**Year 1075.** 1075 was the first dry year at Asellby and not the last. And what Asellby had stored was gone by the second summer. The water that was left at Asellby went where water always goes, which is to whoever owns the channel, and the clerks headed the year the Drought of 1075.

**Year 1075.** What turned up at Ramwill Reach was worth more than the year it turned up in. The assessment at Ramwill Reach caught up within three years. For a generation Ramwill Reach was somewhere to arrive at; the entry is the Ramwill Reach Find, filed under fortune.

**Year 1100.** The throne passed quietly to Bravere, and the peaceful ones are the short entries, and Bravere got a short entry. Neither the ledger nor the map at Asellby records that Bravere arrived.

**Year 1100.** The terms that ended it were written at Asellby, and the magnates ceded nothing but their claim and kept their ledger, which held little, which is what the Crown had come to Asellby for. What the Crown took at Asellby it had already won; the Peace of Asellby is the title on the copy kept at Asellby.

**Year 1100.** Ramwill Reach found something and did not have to build it, and the wagons came to Ramwill Reach before anyone official did. The money reached Ramwill Reach before the assessors, which is the only sequence that ever helps a town. The Ramwill Reach Find is how it is filed, without a description.

**Year 1100.** Asellby conceded the Crown Granary: it taxes the fat years to buy bread for the lean ones, and it is the first decree in the history of Asellby to move coin downhill. Nothing in the Asellby register credits anyone with having been right earlier.

**Year 1100.** Glasholt Crag rose in 1100 and was not put down. What Glasholt Crag makes stays at Glasholt Crag. People came to Glasholt Crag from the tariffed country around it, which is the plainest verdict available; the date is kept at Glasholt Crag as the Glasholt Crag Rising.

**Year 1125.** The money that had built Asellby was withdrawn from it. And the wire to Asellby was never taken up, and nothing was ever sent along it again. The charter for Asellby is still on file and refers to nothing standing.

**Year 1125.** The water came through Ramwill Reach in 1125 and stayed a fortnight. Nobody at Ramwill Reach was compensated and nobody had expected to be. The count of the dead at Ramwill Reach is an estimate and always was, so the water year is entered as the Ramwill Reach Flood.

**Year 1125.** A storm out of the open water found Asellby in 1125. The shipping at Asellby was lost at anchor. What Asellby lost was a season, and a season is what it lives on. The Great Storm of 1125 is the name that stuck.

**Year 1150.** The Osmark Combine arrived at Ramwill Reach Harbor with money instead of soldiers. The yards at Ramwill Reach kept every worker and changed every owner. The ledger that governs Ramwill Reach is kept where nobody in Ramwill Reach can read it.

**Year 1150.** The calls at Hamwick Harbor became regular in 1150, so an invitation is cheaper than a fleet, and Hamwick Harbor is being invited. Asellby saw the envoys and recorded nothing.

**Year 1150.** Hamwick Harbor was the landing, and 4 regions followed it. The assessment moved to the water at Hamwick Harbor and stayed there. The 4 occupied regions are the best-connected and worst-paid ground in the realm. And it is entered as the Landing at Hamwick in a language Hamwick does not use.

**Year 1150.** The First Magnate died in 1150, and the transfer of the chair took a fortnight. And nobody in the 12 regions was consulted, and nobody expected to be. Nothing about the arrangement over 12 regions turned on whether the name was Vathrek.

**Year 1150.** The water threw a storm at Asellby and held it there, and what Asellby lost was a season, and a season is what most of it lives on. Nothing was rebuilt at Asellby that did not earn its rebuilding: the clerks entered it as the Great Storm of 1150.

**Year 1150.** A find was made at Ramwill Reach that nobody had budgeted for; for one generation Ramwill Reach was worth going to. The people who had already left Ramwill Reach did not come back, so the register calls it the Ramwill Reach Find.

**Year 1175.** Asellby chartered what the magnates had already begun at Hamwick, and the copper reached Hamwick on the strength of an assay. Nothing was asked of Hamwick and nothing was offered to it either.

**Year 1175.** The throne stood empty for a season after the Sovereign died, and the clerks at Asellby recorded the outcome and not the method. The title at Asellby changed to Lasellmoor and the arrangement did not.

**Year 1175.** The water table under Asellby dropped below the wells: the wells that had been common at Asellby stopped being common. Two dry years is not a disaster in Asellby's ledgers and is one at Asellby, and the Drought of 1175 is the name and the whole of the explanation.

**Year 1200.** The old Hierarch died, and the succession was contested. And the rivals to Corisonne are not named here, which is itself a kind of record. The 12 regions were informed by notice that Corisonne had the title.

**Year 1200.** The river took the bottom of Ramwill Reach. The survey had marked it and the leases at Ramwill Reach were signed anyway. The leases at Ramwill Reach still run to the water: it is the Ramwill Reach Flood in the register and something shorter in Ramwill Reach.

**Year 1200.** The wind held onto the Asellby shore and would not let go of it. And the exposure that makes Asellby a harbour is the exposure that emptied it. The exposure that makes a harbour is the exposure that empties it, at Asellby as everywhere. The Great Storm of 1200 is the year in every account that survives.

**Year 1225.** Something under Hamwick turned out to be worth carrying, so what was found at Hamwick outvalued everything built there. What was found at Hamwick was worth more than everything anyone had built there; the Hamwick Find is the entry and the entry says nothing else.

**Year 1250.** The Osmark Combine left Ramwill Reach in 1250, 100 years after it opened the concession. What had been built at Ramwill Reach was left standing and left cold. Ramwill Reach kept the buildings and lost the market, and kept the ground and lost the levy.

**Year 1250.** The First Magnate died in 1250 and left no undisputed heir, and while the court fought itself the gates went unwatched, and the rivals of Asellby moved in. Asellby kept the date and the name Kravzeth and dropped everything else.

**Year 1250.** The streams above Gnokton were down to stones by 1250. And what water there was went where it had always gone, which at Gnokton meant uphill. The grain price at Gnokton doubled and the wage did not: the year went into the register as the Drought of 1250.

**Year 1250.** Ramwill Reach was under water to the second street; everything stored at the Ramwill Reach wharves was a loss, and the stores were the year. The river will do it again, and the Ramwill Reach leases still run to the water, and the accounts call it the Ramwill Reach Flood, and they do not agree on how many it took.

**Year 1250.** Hamwick came into a piece of luck in 1250. For one generation Hamwick was worth the journey. The road to Hamwick was repaired inside a year, which fifty years of petitions had not managed: it goes into the register as the Hamwick Find.

Bynames settled on the places the years used hardest: Fellton the Shadowed, Dravzek Crag the Lofted, Khekton the Hollow, Ramwill Reach the Yoked, Glasholt Crag the Free, Asellby the Steadfast, Brackby the Yoked, Serrvarnuk Bastion the Hollow, Hamwick the Yoked, High Marton the Lofted and Calvermere the Yoked, though those 11 bynames were granted by no charter and can be lifted by no decree, which makes them the plainest record in this document.

## The State of the Realm, Year 1250

By the last epoch, of 9 settled regions, 1 gained, 7 ended where the founding left them and 1 went down. And the maps still name all 3 deadholds, but the roads have stopped going there.

Khekton, Glasholt Crag and Serrvarnuk Bastion are deadholds now: 3 holdings stand where towns used to.

Hamwick rose further than any other place in the record: what rose was the value of the ground, and Hamwick happened to be standing on it.

The realm divides 7 to the Crown, 3 to the Temple and 1 to the magnates, and 0 are contested between them, and 1 answers to no one at all.

4 gates stand in the realm, held 0 Crown, 2 Temple, 0 magnate; a levy at a gate is not a price, because a price implies a choice, and the 4 offer none.

What the gates collected went mostly to the Temple. And the loop is closed: gates pay for gates, and the Crown and the Temple are the pair the capital watches.

The owners' row is 3.9 in the hundred and holds 41.6 in the hundred: 17.7 times better is not a figure of speech, it is the ratio, and at Asellby it is worse.

Over all of it stands the Dominion, which holds 4 regions from its foothold at Hamwick and calls the arrangement trade, though the Crown still reigns, and it no longer rules the 4 regions the Dominion holds, and the two are not the same thing.

3 settlements end the record unwired, and a projection drew that line and 3 settlements fell the wrong side of it.

## What the Record Shows

The record closes with what the numbers say about 12 regions. This world held its shape: the wealth gap stayed at 0.30; the founding roll set 0.29 and the centuries did the rest. The Crown Granary in 1100 is where the curve bends. Read as periods rather than years, it runs the Age of the Gates (1000–1050, gini 0.39 → 0.31), the Age of Tariffs (1075–1100, gini 0.30 → 0.34), the Still Years (1125–1250, gini 0.28 → 0.30). Nothing chose those boundaries but the turns of the gini itself, 0.29 to 0.30. Counted over inhabited towns only, the bottom fifth carries 1.0× the top fifth's blight. At 1.0× the burden is not sorted by wealth at all. Wealth and blight both cluster, at 0.055 and -0.103, where no structure would return -0.091, and treat it as a property of the generated map: smoothing kernels put correlation into the terrain before the first town was placed (p 0.125 / 0.495). A median settlement behind the Dhulzar is 12% poorer than one the same distance out in the open. Nothing but the Dhulzar separates the two sides of it. At the mouth, Ramwill Reach takes delivery of 5 points of other towns' poison, though being last on 12 regions' river is not a policy anyone adopted. At 8 regions the crossing is held by someone the region did not pick, and the tariff is paid anyway. Geology chose the 3 narrow places and whoever held them at the founding still does. Hold distance constant and you get Fellton and High Marton, one open and one walled: Fellton returns wealth 9, market 12, burden 54.3; High Marton returns 5, 27, 56.3, and the difference is the mountain. The size distribution is a rank-size hierarchy, fitted at α 0.81 overall and α 0.60 in the tail: the law was written in at the founding by proportional growth, so it is not evidence of anything; the slope is, at α 0.60 and fit 0.94. 1 coast was wound up when the lode ran thin, and the attention left with the ore. Measured in distance saved, the skyway gives the shadow 76% and the open country 4.3%; the owners' row of the shadow twin measures the wall at 66% less, and its labour still walks the pass.
