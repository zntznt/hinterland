# A Chronicle of the Hinterland

*Kemerby keeps this record of the world called "fix-2", closed in 1250 while Brack reigned.*

## The Founding, Year 1000

The survey that opens this record counts 12 regions and one capital at Kemerby; the south wind and the shape of the rock between them account for most of this record before anyone in it made a choice.

Nobody sized the towns: the largest closed the founding at 11508 and the middling one at 2478, and what a town already had is what it got more of, which is how 11508 pulled that far clear.

The refining is done at Kemerby and consumed where it is made, and the founding left 5 settlements dark, on an arithmetic nobody in them was shown.

Kemerby has no lane and no aerie to reach: nowhere in the realm was the cargo valuable enough or the ground hard enough to justify the stones, though so every crossing between these 12 regions is made on the ground, at the ground's price.

Volkruk Teeth and Molgrek Teeth run across the country and Skal Pass and Bellok Steps are the only way over, though 3 regions sit in the mountains' shadow, cut off from Kemerby by the wall, and it costs them.

The charts put Denmere Expanse off the north coast. And Pellness Harbor won the founding lottery, and the winning ticket was a shoreline.

The Merby Rill runs down from the high ground by Varrgak through 2 regions to the border: no charter allocated the Merby Rill, and the allocation is the steepest thing in this record.

Skavdurk, a delve past Skulgruk, has never been surveyed to the bottom, Lyanth was buried by Varrgak before the founding and has not been disturbed and Korvantgrek, a delve past Vurzekmouth, has never been surveyed to the bottom, and none of it is on any charter, and delving pays at Varrgak when nothing else does, and it is priced accordingly.

The realm's edge at Raven is where Ulvere Quay was allowed to happen: Ulvere Quay is not a failure of the realm's rules, it is what the rules pay for.

There are towers at Raven Tower that no charter permits and no writ reaches; one tower stands against the whole of Kemerby's writ, and the writ has not reached it.

Varrgak Bridge is the only bridge in 12 regions. Whoever came to hold the only span in 12 regions did not have to build it.

## The Years

Cut where the record turns, the years give **the Age of the Gates** (1000–1100), in which the gap did not move, **the Still Years** (1125–1200), when nothing in the spread moved and **the Emptying** (1225–1250), in which the gap did not move. And they are cut where the wealth turned, which is the only boundary the 3 ages actually have.

**Year 1025.** The water closed on Pellness for reasons made elsewhere. Pellness had no say in the quarrel and paid the whole of the settlement. Pellness discovered what a fortune built on somebody else's politics is worth.

**Year 1025.** The ground at Skulgruk was named before anyone asked whether it would hold a life. The loss is a clerical one: Skulgruk was never anybody's home to lose.

**Year 1025.** The sickness reached Kemerby and stayed, so the tax rolls at Kemerby were shorter the following year and were collected in full. The blight at Kemerby was decades old and nobody had been charged for it; the Wasting of 1025 is the heading, and the count under it is an estimate.

**Year 1025.** Whatever the old world left under Skulgruk broke loose, and the Temple calls it a test, the survivors did not, and the blight around Skulgruk has not faded. What the old world left under Skulgruk was not surveyed before it was built over.

**Year 1050.** A lost road, a lode, a relic: the accounts of Hamwiceweld do not agree. What was found at Hamwiceweld outvalued everything built there. The money reached Hamwiceweld before the assessors, which is the only sequence that ever helps a town: the register calls it the Hamwiceweld Find.

**Year 1075.** A seam came up under Hamwiceweld that no chart had: assayers, chancers and retainers were at Hamwiceweld inside a season.

**Year 1075.** Kemerby was where two claims came to blows; the quarrel belonged to the Crown and the magnates and the ground belonged to Kemerby. The capital came out of it a third smaller and a quarter poorer, and the constabulary arrived after the blood and stayed, and it survives as the War of 1075 in both sides' copies.

**Year 1075.** The throne passed to Ulverfolt in 1075, exactly as the charter said it would; the entry at Kemerby runs to one line, and that is generous. In none of the 12 regions is a Sovereign chosen by the people who live under one, and Ulverfolt is no exception.

**Year 1075.** Kemerby put a Tariff Amnesty on the books: the fees at every gate were capped by decree out of Kemerby, and the gates were left standing. Kemerby had heard the argument for years and acted on the arithmetic.

**Year 1075.** The ground at Kemerby was made holy in 1075: the Crown's writ had failed there, and the magnates' ledgers had seen nothing at Kemerby worth the ink. The Temple did not prevent what happened at Kemerby and did consecrate it, and the record keeps both.

**Year 1100.** The Hierarch was buried and Sarelle was installed in the same month, so Sarelle took the arrangement as found and left it as found. The ledger at Kemerby did not notice that a Hierarch had become Sarelle.

**Year 1100.** The paper that ended it was signed at Kemerby. The magnates ceded nothing but their claim and kept their ledger, which held little, which is what the Crown had come to Kemerby for. No clause at Kemerby surprised anyone who had read the accounts; the Peace of Kemerby is the name on the paper.

**Year 1100.** The rising at Skulgruk held. And no gate at Skulgruk has charged anyone since. And it flourished: the aetherworks the charter had held back ran at full tilt, and people came to Skulgruk from the tariffed country around it. The people keep the date as the Skulgruk Rising.

**Year 1125.** By winter the Dominion held 7 regions, and it began at Pellness Harbor, so the quays at Pellness Harbor collect for a power across the sea now. It is the first country in the realm wired end to end, because the cargo of those 7 regions is wanted elsewhere; the Dominion's registers head it the Landing at Pellness.

**Year 1125.** The wells at Sellowford were reading low by midsummer and dry by the next, and the fields nearest the channels held, and everything past them at Sellowford did not. The grain price at Sellowford doubled and the wage did not, so it survives as the Drought of 1125, three lines long.

**Year 1175.** The seam under Kemerby ran out and took the yards with it, and everyone at Kemerby who could leave left, in the order their savings allowed. Nobody at Kemerby was paid to plan for this, and so nobody did.

**Year 1175.** The Hierarch died in 1175, and the transfer of the censer took a fortnight; the 12 regions were told and were not asked. Neither the ledger nor the map at Kemerby records that Santhe arrived.

**Year 1175.** The succession of 1175 was decided by force and recorded as a succession. While the court fought itself the gates went unwatched, and the rivals of Kemerby moved in. The First Magnate changes and the arrangement does not, and Krumth inherits it entire.

**Year 1175.** Vurzekmouth was under water to the second street, so the fields, the founding wharves, whatever stood in the way at Vurzekmouth. The count of the dead at Vurzekmouth is an estimate and always was, and it is entered as the Vurzekmouth Flood.

**Year 1200.** The water failed at Sellowford and kept failing: wells around Sellowford that had been shared were closed off. Two dry years is not a disaster in Kemerby's ledgers and is one at Sellowford. And the dry page is headed the Drought of 1200.

**Year 1200.** The river went through Vurzekmouth rather than past it, and the stores at Vurzekmouth were lost, and the stores were the year. Nobody at Vurzekmouth was compensated, because nobody at Vurzekmouth had insured anything, and it is the Vurzekmouth Flood in the register and something shorter in Vurzekmouth.

**Year 1200.** Markholt felt the rock let go beneath it; the survey had said what the ground under Markholt would do. The masons at Markholt had said which streets would go and had not been asked twice, so the register carries it as the Markholt Quake.

**Year 1225.** Aetherworks rose at Pellness on a fresh charter. And the capital sealed the charter, and the trunk line reached Pellness within the season. The assessment at Pellness rose in the same season as the wages.

**Year 1225.** The Sovereign died, and Brack was already the answer, so the 12 regions carried on as though nothing had happened, because nothing had. Kemerby kept the date and the name Brack and dropped everything else.

**Year 1225.** The water went out of the country around Sellowford; the channels at Sellowford carried what there was to whoever owned them. Nothing at Sellowford was rationed, which is a decision even unmade: the Drought of 1225 is what the dry page is headed.

**Year 1225.** The river rose over Vurzekmouth and took the low ground with it: the count of what the water took at Vurzekmouth was never completed. The survey had the Vurzekmouth floodline on it and the leases ignored it; it is filed as the Vurzekmouth Flood, with the count left blank.

**Year 1225.** The fold beneath Markholt released what it had been holding: nothing about it was a surprise to anyone who had read the survey of Markholt. Nothing under Markholt has settled, and the record does not pretend otherwise, so the year is filed as the Markholt Quake.

**Year 1225.** A find was made at Hamwiceweld that nobody had budgeted for, and people came back to ground at Hamwiceweld they had been leaving. The road to Hamwiceweld was repaired in a year that fifty petitions had not moved. And the Hamwiceweld Find is how it is filed, without a description.

**Year 1250.** Nothing was settled when the Hierarch died, least of all the censer; Novare came out of it holding the censer, which is all this record will commit to. Across 12 regions the only thing that changed was a name, and the name is Novare.

**Year 1250.** Markholt lost streets to the ground itself. Roads cracked, the pass shifted, and what stood on soft ground at Markholt did not stand after. The rebuilding at Markholt used the same ground and the same mortar; it is filed as the Markholt Quake, under weather, which is wrong.

Bynames settled on the places the years used hardest: Kemerby the Yoked, Skulgruk the Free, Sellowford the Yoked, Merholt the Shadowed, Vurzekmouth the Hollow, Varrgak the Yoked, Markholt the Hollow, Raven the Lofted, Asdenby the Yoked, Hamwiceweld the Yoked, Thereby the Yoked and Pellness the Yoked, so nothing in Kemerby authorised those 12 names and nothing in Kemerby can withdraw them.

## The State of the Realm, Year 1250

Counted at the close, the 9 settled regions divide: 2 ended richer than at the founding, 6 ended where the founding left them and 1 failed, and those 3 deadholds were not closed, they were left.

Skulgruk, Vurzekmouth and Markholt are deadholds now: 3 holdings stand where towns used to.

8 regions to the Crown against 1 to the Temple and 0 to the magnates. 2 are contested between them, and 1 answers to no one at all.

There are 5 gates in the realm and they are held 3 by the Crown, 0 by the Temple and 0 by the magnates, though not one of the 5 gates was chosen by the people who pay at it.

What the gates collected went mostly to the Crown, so the loop closes on itself: the Crown and the Temple are simply the two nearest to acting on it.

The sharper division is not between towns but inside them, where 3.6 of the people hold 34.4 of the coin. And they live 14.2 times better than the people who work for them, and the gap is sharpest at Kemerby, where 55 coins in every hundred belong to the few.

7 of the realm's regions answer across the water now, though the Crown still reigns, and it no longer rules the 7 regions the Dominion holds, and the two are not the same thing.

1 settlements are dark at the close and were dark at the founding. And the wire goes where a ledger says it repays, and 1 settlements do not.

## What the Record Shows

What the survey of 12 regions supports, and nothing beyond it. Extraction: the spread opened from 0.27 to 0.36 and the poorest tenth fell: 0.36 is an ordinary reading for this ruleset. A Tariff Amnesty in 1075 is where the curve bends. The run does not read as one period but as 3: the Age of the Gates (1000–1100, gini 0.29 → 0.31), the Still Years (1125–1200, gini 0.30 → 0.31), the Emptying (1225–1250, gini 0.35 → 0.36), and so the 3 ages are cut from the same epoch series the timeline draws, so the two surfaces cannot disagree. The poorest fifth of this realm's towns carries 0.3× the blight of its richest fifth, and so at 0.3× it is the wealthy end carrying the heavier load, which these rules permit and do not require. Spatial clustering returns 0.403 for wealth and -0.266 for blight over the region adjacency; the smoothing kernels that lay down the terrain guarantee some of this before any economy runs, so the number describes the map's construction as much as its history (p 0.020 and 0.135, 199 permutations). A median settlement behind the Volkruk is 95% poorer than one the same distance out in the open, though the Volkruk is the only term left once distance is controlled for. Vurzekmouth stands at the river mouth and takes 9 points of other towns' poison with the water, and so being last on 12 regions' river is not a policy anyone adopted. 8 regions are charged at crossings they do not own; there is no route around the 4 of them; that is what makes them worth holding. One side of the Volkruk gets 38 and the other 2. The farms went where the water was, 38 against 2, and the wind was not consulted. The size distribution came out rank-size, tail slope α 1.07, fit 0.87. Proportional growth guarantees a size law before any economy runs, so read the slope and not the line: fit 0.87, primacy 1.1×. Measured in distance saved, the skyway gives the shadow 57% and the open country 0%, and a lane that saves 57% is a lane priced beyond the people it would save it for.

Nothing above was authored. It fell out of the ore, the wall, the water and what the ledgers said would pay, across 10 epochs.

*The ledger of Kemerby closes further apart and lower down than it opened, though nine parts in the hundred separate the founding spread from the closing one. At the bottom tenth the reading is one, against four at the founding, though the realm is poorer at the close than at the founding by thirty-three parts in the hundred. Nothing in the nine regions was arranged to produce this, which is the part worth sitting with.*
